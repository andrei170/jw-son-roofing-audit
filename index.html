The One Finding That Matters More Than All The Others
A homeowner on a phone cannot contact you

We read your served page source directly. Here is the count:

Forms anywhere on the site 0 Form input fields 0 tel: links (tappable numbers) 0 mailto: links 0 Pages in the menu 3 — Home, Services, About

Roughly three quarters of the people who look at a roofing website are on a phone, usually standing in a garden looking up at a problem. They tap the number. Nothing happens. They then have to memorise eleven digits, close the browser, open the dialler and type it in — or, far more likely, hit back and tap the next roofer on the list.

This is not an SEO problem or an advertising problem. You could rank first tomorrow and still lose most of that traffic, because there is nothing at the end of it to convert. Everything else on this page is secondary to fixing this, and it is a few hours of work.

The Good News, And It Is Genuinely Unusual
Your GA4 tag is already live: G-E6PZPGE4QM

Most trades we audit have no tracking at all, and installing it is the first invoice before anything can be measured. You have already done that part.

What it is not yet doing is counting anything useful — because there are no calls or form submissions for it to count as conversions. Fix the contact routes and your analytics starts producing real numbers immediately, on traffic you are already getting. You would find out within a fortnight what your site is actually worth, using data you have been collecting and not reading.

Checkatrade And The Apps — The Honest Position
You told us you already pay for Checkatrade and other lead apps

Those work by selling the same enquiry to several roofers at once. You are quoting against two or three others on price, on a lead you paid for whether you win it or not. That is a reasonable way to fill a diary and a poor way to build a business, because none of it accrues to you: stop paying and the leads stop the same day.

A Checkatrade lead you rent it, shared, priced against 2-3 rivals Your own website you own it, exclusive, and it compounds Where your reviews live on their platform, not on your site

We would not tell you to cancel them. They pay your wages this month. But every review you have earned on those platforms is currently building their asset rather than yours, and the first job is to make your own site capable of taking an enquiry so there is somewhere for that work to land.

The Title Tag — Ten Minutes, Real Consequence
This is the blue line a searcher reads in Google
What yours says now Jw son roofing What it could say Roofers in Leeds | Repairs, Flat Roofs & Re-Roofing | JW & Son Meta description ✗ none written

Somebody types “roofer Leeds” and gets ten blue links. Yours carries no trade word, no town, and is not even capitalised. It matches nothing they typed.

Related: the site says Leeds head office, UK-wide. For a three-to-five-jobs-a-week roofer, “UK-wide” works against you — Google cannot tell where to rank you, and a homeowner in Headingley reads it and wonders whether you are actually local.

Fix First — This Week, No Spend
  • Make the phone number a tel: link on every page. One line of code. It is the single highest-value change on this list
  • Add a contact page and an enquiry form — roof type, the problem, postcode, name, phone
  • Publish an email address so a landlord or letting agent can send you a job in writing
  • Rewrite the title tag and add a meta description. Ten minutes, and it is your Google headline
Build Next
  • Mark up calls and form fills as GA4 conversions — your tag is already live and counting nothing
  • Ask your Checkatrade customers for a Google review. Move the proof onto ground you own
  • Town pages for Leeds, Headingley, Pudsey, Morley, Horsforth and Wetherby — drop “UK-wide”
  • Publish accreditations and insurance if you hold them, then advertise into a site that converts

You want to double to ten jobs a week. Your website currently takes zero enquiries.

That is not a criticism of the roofing. It means the cheapest growth available to you is sitting in a few hours of work, before anybody spends a pound on advertising.
